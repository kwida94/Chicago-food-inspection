# Restaurant Health Inspection Analysis (2024–2026)
## Overview

In this project, I analyzed restaurant health inspection data from a public city open data portal to better understand compliance trends and risk patterns.

The goal was to clean and structure messy inspection data, then use Excel and Power Query to identify what drives inspection outcomes.

## What I Wanted to Understand

How often inspections pass, require conditions, or fail.

Whether higher-risk facilities fail more often.

How the number of violations affects the outcome.

Which violations occur most frequently.

Whether compliance trends change over time.

## Tools Used

Excel

Power Query

Pivot Tables

Total inspections analyzed: 39,365
Duplicate inspections found: 0

## Key Findings

A large portion of inspections require corrective action or result in failure.

Failure rates increase sharply as violation counts rise. Inspections with 6+ violations are much more likely to fail.

The most common violations relate to sanitation, temperature control, and contamination prevention.

Inspection outcomes remain fairly stable over time, with moderate month-to-month variation.

## What This Suggests

Violation count is a strong indicator of inspection risk.

Core operational practices (cleanliness and food handling) drive most compliance issues.

Risk categories alone don’t fully explain failure patterns.

## Recommendations

Based on analysis:

Focus operator training on sanitation and temperature control.

Use violation-count thresholds to trigger proactive intervention.

Monitor repeat offenders for targeted follow-ups.

Provide seasonal readiness guidance prior to high-risk periods.
